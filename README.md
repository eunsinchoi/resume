## Key Research Achievements During My Master's Program

### Cross-Dataset Robust Audio-Based Depression Detection

During my master's program, I focused on cross-dataset generalization for audio-based depression detection. While many existing studies primarily aim to achieve high performance within a single dataset, I focused on the performance degradation that occurs in real-world scenarios when speaker characteristics, recording conditions, and data collection environments differ across datasets.

To address this issue, I utilized Audio Mamba-based audio embeddings to effectively capture temporal characteristics in speech signals. For speech transcripts, I applied LLM-based Symptom-Guided Representations, where an LLM analyzes utterances for depression-related information and generates symptom-specific representations. This approach was designed to provide the model with clinically meaningful information beyond conventional text embeddings.

By combining non-verbal acoustic features with symptom-related information extracted from speech content, I developed an audio-based depression detection framework designed to maintain robust performance across different datasets. The research considered not only predictive performance but also robustness in real-world environments and the interpretability of model predictions.

### 1st Place — DCASE 2025 Task 6: Language-Based Audio Retrieval

I participated in the IEEE AASP DCASE 2025 Task 6: Language-Based Audio Retrieval and achieved 1st place in the challenge. The task focuses on retrieving audio clips that semantically match natural-language queries, requiring effective representation and alignment between audio and textual modalities.

Our system achieved an mAP@16 score of 0.421, ranking 1st among all participating systems. This achievement demonstrated the practical effectiveness of my experience in audio representation learning and multimodal data processing in a competitive international benchmark.
